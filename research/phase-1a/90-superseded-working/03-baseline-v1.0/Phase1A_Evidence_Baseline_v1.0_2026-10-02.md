## 2. Key evidence findings retained

### Chi Lin Nunnery — P05/P06

P05 provided unusually direct evidence of digital simulated assembly and collision checking. It supported treating assembly validation separately from ordinary model generation or construction animation.

### Yingxian Wooden Pagoda — P07/P08

P07 supported parametric component generation, historical-rule embedding, invisible-dimension inference, and application to a whole-tower HBIM.

P08 documented a 32,000+ component whole-tower model while also limiting interpretation: the model represented an idealized state; some objects were manually modeled; hidden joints could require X-ray inspection or disassembly to confirm.

### Jinci Shengmu Hall — P14

P14 was downgraded to bibliographic / metadata-level confidence for technical claims where original full-text verification was not available. Technical claims that exceeded accessible evidence were to remain UNKNOWN.

### Chuzu'an Hall — P18

P18 provided strong whole-building HBIM evidence:
- 66 component types / 330 families;
- point-cloud-related geometric fitting reported at ≤3 mm;
- internal connections required repair drawings, historical data, and professional research beyond point cloud alone;
- manual family creation remained significant.

Its 45-stage TimeLiner virtual construction sequence was identified as construction-sequence simulation, not proof of physical mortise-tenon assembly validation.

### Zhenwu Pavilion — P19

P19 provided a meaningful engineering-validation chain through HBIM → IFC → SAP2000 structural analysis. This was treated separately from physical assembly validation.

### Rule-driven HBIM–IFC — P21

P21 supported machine-readable relationship / topology expression through HBIM and IFC. Relationship expression was not treated as equivalent to physical assembly validation.

### Patent — P24

P24 remained a technical claim / patent source rather than independent validation.

## 3. Five validation classes

1. Geometric validation
2. Connection / topology-expression validation
3. Physical assembly validation
4. Historical / literature validation
5. Structural / engineering validation

Strict distinctions:

- machine-readable connection/topology expression ≠ physical assembly validation;
- construction sequence / virtual construction ≠ mortise-tenon assembly-constraint validation;
- rule-based generation ≠ proven collision-free assembly;
- complete building model ≠ building-level deterministic regeneration.

## 4. Cross-case findings

Within the P01–P24 evidence range:

- Chinese timber digital-reconstruction precedents exist.
- Multiple cases support separating measured geometry, historical rules, and inferred/unknown information.
- Whole-building HBIM is feasible, but whole-building HBIM alone does not prove deterministic regeneration.
- Hidden connection information is a recurring evidence problem.
- Component libraries, parametric families, procedural generation, ontology/knowledge representation, and IFC relationship modeling solve different parts of the problem and should not be conflated.
- Local or component-level success does not prove whole-building scalability.

## 5. Residual problems identified in audit

1. Five validation classes were defined but not consistently applied across every P01–P24 case.
2. P16, P18, and P19 still risked over-crediting construction/generation logic as physical assembly validation.
3. The eight Deep Dive clusters still needed explicit Claim → Source → Locator → Scope traceability.
4. Phase 1B governance language had at times overreached from READY to APPROVED.
5. Negative search claims needed to be explicitly bounded to the P01–P24 / verified evidence range.

## 6. Open risks

P04, P12, P13, and P14 remained important evidence gaps.

UNKNOWN was not itself a failure condition; unknowns had to remain explicit and not be silently converted into FACT.
