## 4. Paper-level thesis

The paper proposes using **足材 (M)** rather than `份` as a central modular unit for interpreting early timber architecture. For Zhenguo Temple Main Hall it argues that this model can compete with existing integer-chi or fen-based explanations at similar fitting error.

The paper explicitly emphasizes:
- arch/gong length as a key scale indicator;
- distinction between real length and centerline length;
- directionality of dou blocks;
- two-direction gong/fang interactions;
- shadow-gong configuration and eave-rafter span;
- two distinct gong-length groups at Zhenguo Temple.

These are interpretation-level claims, not direct survey facts unless separately present in SRC-ZG-WF-001.

## 7. Section 3.6｜Direction of intersection (critical for current MP-01B review)

S15 explicitly introduces directionality of small dou blocks and orthogonal gong interaction.

### 7.1 General small-dou placement rule discussed by S15

For many early timber examples, S15 observes that small dou have unequal dimensions in the two horizontal directions. The long side of a small dou is generally aligned with the body direction of the gong below it.

Classification:

`S15 INTERPRETATION / EARLY-TIMBER COMPARATIVE OBSERVATION`

### 7.2 Zhenguo Temple special case at a centered second-jump huagong head

S15 says that the detailed survey report provides an important Zhenguo Temple observation for the second-jump huagong head in a `计心` node:

- the small dou is placed **crosswise**;
- its long side is **perpendicular to the huagong body**;
- equivalently, the long side follows the **upper transverse gong** direction.

S15 uses this to explain how orthogonally crossing gong/fang members can achieve a coherent centerline relation.

Paper locator: Section 3.6 `交互的方向`, around p.58.

### 7.3 S15's normalized wording

S15 summarizes the directional rule as:

- `长开斗` long side follows the lower gong/member body;
- `交互斗` long side follows the upper transverse gong direction.

This distinction matters because it shows that the bracket-set system is explicitly **two-directional**, and that member/dou orientation changes at orthogonal intersections.

## 8. Expression 3 / 4｜Horizontal-gong direction vs huagong direction

## 11. Current MP-01B orientation question — what S15 does and does not establish

### Supported by S15

1. Early Zhenguo bracket systems are explicitly treated as **two-directional** systems.
2. `横栱方向` and `华栱方向` are distinct axes.
3. Linggong belongs to the horizontal/transverse-gong family in S15's terminology.
4. At an orthogonal centered interaction, dou orientation can rotate so that its long side follows the upper transverse gong rather than the huagong below.
5. Therefore the current Gate G assumption `Interior Linggong local +X → assembly +Y` is **not historically proven merely because the Pingliang is drawn along +Y**.

### Not established by S15 alone

1. The exact long-axis direction of the **interior** Linggong in the target chain `四椽栿 → 驼峰 / 令栱 → 平梁`.
2. Whether the target Linggong is centered on the Pingliang endpoint, inward-shifted, or arranged as another local bracket composition.
3. Exact target-node contact-face geometry.
4. Hidden mortise/tenon/groove topology.
5. Whether the target interior Linggong shares the same dimensional family as the exterior small Linggong.
